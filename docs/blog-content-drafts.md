## Draft 1: Creatine Monohydrate

### Creatine Monohydrate: What It Does, When to Take It, and Who It Is For

Creatine has earned its reputation because it is simple, practical, and heavily researched. For people who lift, sprint, train hard, or want to support strength over time, creatine monohydrate is one of the clearest starting points in sports nutrition.

Creatine helps your body maintain phosphocreatine stores, which are involved in rapid energy production during short, intense efforts. That is why it is most often discussed around strength training, repeated sprint work, power output, and lean-mass-focused routines.

### What creatine supports

Creatine is not a stimulant. You should not expect it to feel like caffeine or a pre-workout. Its value is quieter: daily use supports the internal stores your muscles draw from during hard work.

Most people use creatine to support:

- Strength training output
- Repeated high-intensity efforts
- Lean mass goals alongside resistance training
- Training consistency over time

### When to take creatine

The most important part is consistency. Creatine can be taken before training, after training, or with a meal. A simple daily habit beats a complicated schedule.

Many people take 3 to 5 grams per day. Some choose a loading phase, but it is not required for a practical routine. If you are new to creatine, start simple and make it easy to repeat.

### What to look for on the label

Look for a formula that is straightforward, clearly dosed, and free from unnecessary extras. If the goal is creatine, the label should make that obvious.

Clean label checklist:

- Clearly listed creatine form
- Transparent serving size
- No proprietary blend hiding the dose
- No unnecessary colors or filler ingredients

### Where creatine fits in a stack

Creatine pairs naturally with protein, hydration, and a training plan built around progressive overload. It does not replace any of those things. It works best as part of a routine that already respects sleep, nutrition, and consistent training.

## Draft 2: Pre-Workout Ingredients

### Pre-Workout Ingredients Explained: Citrulline, Beta-Alanine, Caffeine, and More

A good pre-workout should make sense before you ever scoop it. The best place to start is the ingredient panel: what is included, why it is there, and whether it matches the way you train.

### Citrulline

Citrulline is commonly used in pre-workout formulas to support nitric oxide production and blood flow during training. It is most relevant for people chasing hard sets, pump-focused sessions, and sustained training quality.

### Beta-alanine

Beta-alanine is often associated with the tingling sensation some people feel after taking pre-workout. Its main use case is repeated high-intensity work, especially when sets or intervals push into a demanding duration.

### Caffeine

Caffeine is the familiar one. It can support alertness, energy, and focus, but more is not automatically better. The right caffeine dose depends on tolerance, training time, sleep, and how sensitive you are to stimulants.

### Electrolytes

Electrolytes matter when sweat, heat, or long sessions enter the picture. A pre-workout routine that ignores hydration can feel strong on paper and weak in practice.

### How to choose

Choose based on your training context:

- Morning lift: moderate caffeine may fit.
- Evening lift: lower-stim or stim-free may make more sense.
- Pump-focused session: prioritize citrulline and hydration.
- Conditioning work: consider electrolytes and repeat-effort support.

The right pre-workout is not the loudest formula. It is the formula you can use consistently without fighting your sleep, stomach, or training plan.
